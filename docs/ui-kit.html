The deck

Every card is built from the same parts: cream stock, a double gold rule, Fraunces indices and the illustrated suits. Each card sizes itself from its own width, so one card works as a tiny chip in a meld or as a full face for print (63 × 88 mm, poker size). Court cards are double-ended: one portrait, mirrored, so they read the right way up from either side of the table. Tap any card or figure to see it full size.

    Screens

    Every screen of the app on a phone, then tablets, desktops, the TV and a big night with several tables.

    The rework, 17 November

    The app was remade against one brief: it replaces pencil and paper, so every moment has to be faster than writing eight names on a sheet and ruling a grid. Phone first, a tablet on the table a very close second. These are the screens as built. Where an older frame further down disagrees with this section, this section is right.

    1. Ask nothing up front that can be defaulted or asked later. A new table is dealt a May I? name (The Cheeky Jokers, Three of a Kind). Results offers to keep it, roll another or type the table's own, after the first game.
    2. Nothing pops up when the hand is dealt. The round guide opens when tapped, or every round for tables that switch it on.
    3. One main action a screen. While a game is going, Home's game card is the way back in and the dock is only the tabs. With nothing in play the dock holds New game.
    4. Nothing covers a face, and nothing rests under the main button. A buy is confirmed in place of the hint line.
    5. The app notices what paper can't. A score no hand could hold is asked about once, never blocked.
    6. The score sheet is always one tap away, mid-game and after. On a tablet the running totals sit under every face.
    7. Everything that looks tappable works. Every plus, face, dealer tag and empty seat is a real button of at least 44px.
    8. Light comes from the corners. Two soft pools of the look's own colour, top left and bottom right, breathing slowly. Nothing circles through the middle of the screen, and no gold is mixed into the water.
    9. The crest opens the app; Play belongs to the table. The opening lasts about a second, plays once a visit, skips on a tap and never plays for anyone who asked for less motion. Play carries a small mark instead of a hero, so what is on it is the game in progress, the last game's moment, the tables and the awards.
    10. The main button never matches its ground. Cream on the water, the look's colour on a cream sheet. The second button goes quiet.
    11. The tab bar is a platform size. About 72px tall with 34px art, between iOS (49pt) and Android (80dp). Big pictures belong to the page above it.
    12. Rules belong to the table. House rules are a line on the names screen before the first deal, a row in the New game sheet for every game after, and a row in the game menu while playing. The same sheet opens from all three, and whatever a game is dealt with is where the table's next game starts.
    13. One frame for every place. The start screen, Home, a table, a new table, Learn, Stats and Settings share one frame: the main button, then Learn, Play, Stats and Settings in exactly the same spot. Settings is a tab, so no screen carries a gear for it. The first visit and the hundredth look the same; only what is on the table changes.
    14. One job, one layout. Seating players is one list, for the first table and every table after it. The guide is one page.
    15. Game screens stay focused; the guide is always in the same corner. There are no tabs during a game. The Learn book sits in the top bar of the round, the score pad and between rounds, and Back returns to exactly where the table was. The score pad shows what each card is worth.

    The round

    Headers are part of the background: a page or round title, its round name and its art sit straight on the teal, never in a floating box or card. Three layout sections, in the order a night is played: the round screen, here; then the game start to finish (score the round, between rounds, results and the TV); then home and the table. Every screen is built from the components above and carries one piece of art at hero size. Who's leading is always the same Leaderboard, between rounds, on the TV, at the end of the night and on the table page.

    The art size ladder

    Every screen carries at least one hero. A player is never smaller than a token, and a token is never the only art on a screen.

    How the leaderboard works

    • It answers one question, who's leading, and appears only between rounds, on the TV, in Results and on the table page. Never during a round.
    • One row per player, best first: position, face, name, that player's highlight, and the total in big figures. Nothing else.
    • The leader wears the crown and their figures turn gold. A tie shares the position, the crown and the gold.
    • A small ▲ or ▼ beside the position says who moved this round. No gaps, no "points behind".
    • D beside a name marks who deals next.
    • Two to eight players. Past six the rows tighten; on the TV they grow. Never colour alone: every row writes the name.

    The round screen

    • Four things, top to bottom: the round and its goal, the May I? buys, and the way out of the round. Who dealt and who goes first are marked on their faces.
    • The goal is drawn with real cards from the deck. Tap it for the round guide with the full rules.
    • The faces sit in seat order round an oval table. D marks the dealer and a gold 1st marks who goes first. Two to six sit three across; seven or eight sit four across with smaller faces.
    • Each tap on a face records one buy and shows an Undo for five seconds. The limit is the house rule, 3 by default. At the limit the face reads "No buys left" and can't be tapped.
    • "Someone went out" is the only teal button. Players, Fix an earlier round, rules and settings live in the game menu (the menu sheet itself is not drawn in the kit yet).
    • No leaderboard, no totals and no turn tracking here: the app can't see whose turn it is, so it doesn't pretend to.

    Leaderboard rules

    • Before round 1 is saved there is no leaderboard; the round screen is all there is.
    • Long names truncate in the row; the full name is still read out by screen readers.
    • Under calm mode and reduced motion the rows appear in place; nothing deals in.

      Next round

      Between rounds. One headline moment, then the Leaderboard: best first, the leader crowned in gold, each row carrying that player's highlight of the round, so nothing is said twice. Then the next round as the kit's round guide card (swipe for the rounds after), and one teal "Let's play"; Undo is a text link.

      Score pad rules

      • One screen. Every player is a row in seat order: face, name, Out, the points field and a cards button.
      • Out marks the one player who went out. Their field locks at 0. Tapping Out on someone else moves it.
      • The points field is the default: type and move on. The cards button opens Tap cards for that hand, with Photo beside it, and sends the total back to the row.
      • Under each name: the old total and the new one, so a typo shows before it's saved.
      • Save round 4 stays off until every hand has a number and one player is out, or "Nobody went out" is chosen. A line above the dock says what's missing.
      • Card values: 2 to 9 at face value, 10 J Q K at 10, Ace and Joker at the house value, 15 by default.

      Highlights on the leaderboard

      • Every player gets one highlight a round, drawn only from what the app knows: their score, who went out, buys used, and places before and after.
      • The set includes: still leading, a new leader, the biggest and smallest hand, up or down places, off the bottom, a buy and a climb in the same round.
      • Each is the award art at 24px and a few words, under the name. The headline already says who went out, so their row says something else.
      • Kind words for bad rounds: "Caught with 25", never a jab.

      Buttons

      • The main button is the one next step on a screen, and it always contrasts with what it sits on: cream on the water, the look's colour on a cream sheet. A second choice goes quiet: dark glass with a gold edge on the water (Share beside Rematch), cream on paper. A text link is the way back or the small fix (Undo, Nobody went out, Clear). Ruby only appears inside a menu, for Delete.

      Motion

      • A buy (450ms). The face pops to 1.1× and the next jewel fills; the Undo toast drops in.
      • Out (300ms). The stamp drops from 1.3× to 1×; the row's field settles on 0.
      • The leaderboard (about 0.6s). Rows deal in from the top, 70ms apart; the crown drops onto the leader last.
      • The last round (1.6s). The winner's card rises, the crown drops last, then the rest of the table is dealt in below, 90ms apart.
      • Reduced motion and calm mode: everything appears in place with a 200ms fade, nothing flies, no sparkle.

            How to play
            1

            Lowest score wins

            Seven rounds. After each one you count the cards left in your hand, and the lowest total after round 7 wins.

            2

            Sets

            A set is three cards of the same rank. The suits don't matter.

            3

            Runs

            as 10♥

            A run is four cards in a row, all one suit. A joker can fill the gap.

            4

            The round's goal

            Round 2 · Set & Run
            5♣5♦5♥8♥9♥10♥J♥

            Each round asks for its own sets and runs. Lay the whole goal down in one turn, once a round.

            5

            May I?

            +1 May I?

            Want someone else's discard? Call May I? and take it with one penalty card from the stock. Three buys a round.

            6

            Going out

            Play your last card and you're out. You score nothing for the round, and it ends for everyone.

            7

            Counting up

            510101515

            Everyone else adds up their hand: 2 to 9 at face value, 10 J Q K at 10, Ace and Joker at 15.

                        Big night rules

                        • A night belongs to people, not phones. Each table's phone records its own games. The night joins them up by face.
                        • Looks are unique for the night: same card is fine, same card in the same colour is not. Photos and initials always count as unique.
                        • Every game earns 5 for a win, 3 for 2nd, 2 for 3rd and 1 for playing, at any table size. Ties at the end go to more wins, then the lowest average score.
                        • Between games the default is Up and down. Tables stay at five, and the host can drag anyone.
                        • Late arrivals take any open seat. If none is free, they join the next game. Leaving early keeps your points.
                        • No phone at a table can end the night. Only the host's phone shows End the night, and it comes with an Undo.

                        Photo into a card

                        • Done on the phone: cut out the face, ink the edges, map it to two warm deck colours, then add a crown, collar and mirrored half.
                        • It's predictable, free and private, which matters with kids at the table. An AI redraw could look richer, but it costs per try, sends faces away and can come back strange.
                        • If we ever add an AI version, it should be optional, run on photos of grown-ups only, show a preview before saving and always offer this phone version as well.
