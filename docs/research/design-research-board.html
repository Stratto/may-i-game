May I? · design research

What great game and app design is doing, and three new directions for May I?

Pop, Midnight and Fresh each changed the paint but kept the same app underneath. The research points to a different fix: give the app a world and characters, and make every tap feel physical. Each direction below is a live prototype, so tap the buttons.

What the best references do

BalatroCard game · multiple Game of the Year awards

Every scoring moment stacks several kinds of feedback at once: cards bounce, numbers roll, particles burst and the screen shakes. The shake gets stronger with the size of the score.

For us: make a big round score feel big, and an Out feel like a win. Keep it all small enough to switch off.
Is This Seat Taken?2026 Apple Design Award, Delight and Fun

It uses simple, soft-shaded characters with expressive faces. The charm comes from the characters, not from decoration.

For us: the players are the content. Give each one a character that reacts to how their game is going.
Marvel SnapCard game · award-winning touch UI

The cards are the hero objects, and every interaction is quick and direct. Little text, a clear next action, and moments of drama only at the reveal.

For us: one obvious next action per screen, with drama saved for the end of a round.
Scorekeeper apps (Scory, TallyPoints, Skorer)Our direct competitors

Each player has their own colour and avatar used everywhere, haptics confirm every entry, and there are two views: quick play and a detailed sheet.

For us: players' identities are what tie the app together.
2026 interface trendsAgency trend reports

Restrained, tactile depth and textures are back. So is purposeful motion, with warnings that too much animation quickly gets irritating.

For us: things should feel like real objects you can press, without fake gloss.

Principles for the rebuild

  • Players are charactersEach player has an avatar that runs through every screen and reacts to the game.
  • One action per momentEach phase shows one big, obvious next step in thumb reach.
  • Feedback scales with the momentA small score gets a tick, an Out gets a celebration, and the final result gets the big show.
  • Flat, not heavyNo deep or offset shadows. Depth comes from colour and layout, and buttons give a gentle squeeze when tapped.
  • Readable across the tableScores stay large, have high contrast and line up in columns. They work in sunlight and at a glance.
  • Calm by default, switchableRespect Reduce Motion, keep haptics subtle, and let players turn the effects down.

Three directions

A · Paper Table

Looks like a printed score pad: two-colour riso ink, paper grain, slightly tilted rows and a rubber stamp when someone goes out. Tactile, warm and grown-up, with something handmade about it.

Tap a row to stamp it OUT

B · Little Table

Inspired by Is This Seat Taken?, every player is a soft character sitting around the table. Their faces change with their scores, and they hop when they go out. The most charming and social of the three.

Tap a character to make them go out

C · High Stakes

A toned-down take on Balatro's game feel: deep felt, poker-chip avatars, rolling numbers and a screen shake that scales with the score. The most game-like, with a lot of drama in each round.

Tap "Add 25" or "Add 90" to feel the shake
